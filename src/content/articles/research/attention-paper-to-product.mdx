## The question

How can a sequence model connect information across positions without processing the sequence through a recurrent hidden state?

Vaswani and colleagues introduced the Transformer in [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762), first submitted in June 2017. This article explains their work and distinguishes it from the teaching examples below.

## The core idea

Attention combines information from different positions with input-dependent weights. The original Transformer also includes positional information, feedforward layers, residual paths and normalization; its architecture is described in [section 3 of the paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7#S3).

## What the paper reports

<ResearchStatement
  kind="source"
  source="https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7#S6"
  sourceLabel="Vaswani et al., sections 4 and 6"
>
  <p>
    The authors report translation results on WMT 2014 English–German and
    English–French, and additional English constituency-parsing evaluations.
    They compare translation quality and training cost with the models listed in
    the paper. Section 4 analyzes the parallelization of self-attention relative
    to recurrence.
  </p>
</ResearchStatement>

## What the evidence supports

The reported translation comparisons support the authors’ claims under the evaluated datasets and training configurations. Their [parsing experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7#S6.SS3) examine an additional task. These are results reported by the original authors, not measurements performed by TheJobPoolConsultants.

## What those results do not establish

<ResearchStatement kind="limitation">
  <p>
    The evaluated tasks do not establish universal superiority across every
    dataset, sequence length, compute budget or deployment constraint. The paper
    also does not establish that an attention weight is a causal explanation of
    an individual prediction. That is a limit on what can be concluded here, not
    a separate experiment reported by this publication.
  </p>
</ResearchStatement>

## Engineering interpretation

<ResearchStatement kind="interpretation">
  <p>
    One engineering interpretation is to examine the paths through which a
    representation can receive information. A direct attention connection offers
    a different path from recurrent state propagation. The masks, input
    selection and surrounding architecture still constrain what information can
    be used.
  </p>
</ResearchStatement>

The following is an engineering exercise, not a new experimental result.

<ResearchStatement kind="example">
  <p>
    Imagine the sentence “The parcel beside the boxes arrived late.” Treat
    “parcel” and “arrived” as positions you want to connect in a teaching
    diagram. Draw a row for each token and the permitted connections between
    rows. This invented sentence illustrates information paths; it is not an
    observed attention map from a trained model.
  </p>
</ResearchStatement>

Then consider a causal decoder: with shifted target inputs, masking prevents access to future target tokens. Drawing that boundary helps distinguish available information from information a model actually uses.

## Paper → Product

If you use a Transformer through an API, this mental model can guide design questions. What material is supplied in the context? Which interactions are permitted? What happens when needed evidence is missing or truncated?

## Proposed experiment

<ResearchStatement kind="experiment">
  <p>
    Design a question whose answer depends on one controlled passage. Keep the
    model version, question and generation settings fixed. Move the passage
    through the context and add controlled distractors. Include a version with
    the evidence removed. Record answer support and variability over repeated
    runs before drawing conclusions.
  </p>
  <p>This is a proposed experiment. No results are claimed here.</p>
</ResearchStatement>

## Open questions

    useful architecture choice depends on that context, not on the name of the
    model family.
  </p>
</ResearchStatement>
