# Inside AI Systems article brief

Series: **Inside AI Systems — One Problem, One Architecture, One Lesson.**

Keep one practical problem and one primary learning outcome. Aim for a substantial explanation that gives students enough information to trace the mechanism and complete the exercise; length alone is not the quality gate.

## Metadata

Use the existing article schema: unique slug, canonical topic/subtopics/tags, `series: inside-ai-systems`, next unique `seriesOrder`, exact existing program ID, difficulty, real local publication date, author `thejobpoolconsultants`, audience, prerequisites, learning outcomes, scoped source records, and valid related-article slugs. Assign the topic's existing `section` ID and an `order` within that section for the shared article sidebar. These fields organize content; they do not record reader state. Keep a new article in draft until complete and reviewed.

## The practical problem

Start with a concrete user task and observable failure. Name what must work and what would count as success. Identify fictional scenarios and proposed designs explicitly.

## Explain the mechanism

Define unfamiliar terms and expand abbreviations on first use. Separate the simplified teaching model from implementation-specific behavior. Cite the primary source near the claim it supports.

## Follow the architecture

Show the main request/data/control path, required trust boundaries, permission and approval gates, storage, failure exits, and any feedback or recovery loop. Give readable labels and a text alternative. Reuse the accessible article architecture component and independently validated viewer where suitable.

## A worked example

Walk through one case step by step. State assumptions before arithmetic. Label invented values. Preserve units, request identities, source versions, and measurement boundaries. Do not turn plausible values into a benchmark claim.

## Decisions and tradeoffs

Explain why each major component exists, what alternative might be simpler, and what evidence would justify additional complexity. Cover observability and evaluation appropriate to the failure.

## Common mistakes and limitations

Give specific mistakes, their consequence, and how a reader can detect them. State what the source evidence and teaching model do not establish.

## Exercise and completion checkpoint

Provide a small accessible task, required inputs, expected observable behavior, and a clear artifact or answer. A paper simulation can be useful when hardware is unavailable. Run every executable example and record its environment; label pseudocode and unrun proposals.

## One lesson to carry forward

State the lesson in concrete terms and connect it to the next complete article. The shared layout supplies source lists, program/enquiry links, and series navigation. Check them in the final rendered page.
